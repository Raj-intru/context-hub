# Agentic Product Management

**A teaching module.** How a product team runs a cycle where agents do the
research, the ranking and the costing, and people make two decisions that nobody
is allowed to automate.

> **Where this comes from.** The cycle taught here — five steps, two gates, and a
> three-part score of cost, time to market and value — is **ENDGAME's Agentic
> Product Management framework**. We teach it; we did not invent it. Credit it by
> name when you present it. What this module adds is the teaching apparatus: the
> scoring made computable, the failure modes, the templates and a run sheet.

## Who it is for

Not the Idea to App learner. That module takes one person with no coding
experience from an idea to a live app in four weeks. **This one is for a team
that already ships** — a product lead, an architect, engineers, and a portfolio
to choose between.

| | Idea to App | Agentic Product Management |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | One person, new to software | A product team that already ships |
| Scope | One app | A portfolio, choosing between options |
| Shape | Five stages, start to finish | A cycle that repeats and feeds itself |
| Gates | Five, signed by the learner | Two, signed by the product lead |
| Ends when | The app is live with five users | It does not end |

They share one idea, and it is the reason they belong in the same programme:
**the agent does the work, and a named human signs the decision.** If you have
taught one, you have taught the vocabulary for the other.

## The cycle in one paragraph

Agents gather signal continuously — market, customers, portfolio, engineering,
analytics, and what the last release actually did. They turn that into one ranked
backlog where a strategic bet, a customer request, a tech debt item and an
analytics finding all compete in the same list. They cost every option in money,
weeks and expected metric movement. Then **a human picks one** — Gate A. Agents
and the team shape the slice, build it, ship it and track it. Then **a human
reviews the outcome** — Gate B. The metrics feed the next cycle.

## The one sentence to open with

> "Everything on the left of the gate is done by agents. Everything at the gate
> is done by a person whose name is on it. The framework is not the agents — the
> agents are the cheap part. The framework is the two places where it stops."

## How to teach it

Ninety minutes, in `workshop.md`. The shape that works:

1. Show the cycle (10 min). Let them see that four kinds of work compete in one list.
2. **Score three real options from their own backlog** (35 min). This is the module. Nothing else lands until they have argued about a number.
3. Run Gate A on those three, out loud (20 min). The lead picks and says why not the other two.
4. Write the Gate B review date and metric before anyone leaves (10 min).
5. The failure modes (15 min). Ask which two they already have.

Do not teach the steps in order and run out of time before the gates. If you have
forty minutes, teach Gate A and the scoring, and put the six agents on one slide.
